Build Plan

Tools required

Power tools

  • Drill press (strongly preferred for consistent mortise angles) or handheld drill
  • Bandsaw or jigsaw (cutting seat disc)
  • Disc or belt sander (shaping seat edge and leg tapers — optional)

Hand tools

  • Brace and bit or auger bit 22 mm (mortise boring)
  • Chisel 22 mm (cleaning mortise walls)
  • Tenon saw (cutting leg lengths)
  • Spokeshave or drawknife (optional leg taper)
  • Marking gauge
  • Bevel gauge (setting splay angle)
  • Sliding bevel or purpose-made boring guide (for angled drilling)
  • Clamps × 3
  • Mallet

Jigs & accessories

  • Boring guide block: a square block with a 22 mm hole drilled at 10° — used to guide the drill at consistent splay
  • Card scraper or sandpaper 80/120/180 grit

Cut list

Seat 1 280 mm dia × 40 mm T Cut from glulam blank or solid slab

Tenon 3 22 mm dia × 32 mm Turned or cut from dowel stock

Leg actual length depends on splay — cut long, dry-fit, trim feet to level.

Build steps

  1. Cut seat disc — Mark centre and a 280 mm circle on the blank. Cut out with bandsaw or jigsaw. Smooth edge to the line with a spokeshave or sanding disc.

  2. Mark mortise positions — From the underside, mark centre. Scribe a circle at 55% of seat radius (≈ 77 mm from centre). Mark three points at 120° intervals (use a protractor or divide with a compass). These are the mortise centres.

  3. Bore mortises — Set bevel gauge to 10°. Using the boring guide block (or drill press tilted 10°), bore each mortise 32 mm deep with a 23 mm spade or auger bit (23 mm gives 1 mm clearance on the 22 mm tenon). Bore toward the outside of the seat so legs splay outward. Clean walls with a chisel.

  4. Make tenons — If using bought 22 mm dowel, cut three 32 mm lengths. If cutting from square stock, round with a shoulder plane or spoke shave to 22 mm diameter over 32 mm length. Test-fit each into its mortise — should push in by hand with light resistance.

  5. Cut legs to rough length — Cut three legs at ~470 mm (10 mm over finished height) to allow final trimming. Mark top and bottom ends.

  6. Glue tenons into legs — Spread exterior PVA on tenon shank and inside mortise. Drive home with a mallet. Wipe squeeze-out immediately.

  7. Check splay and level — Stand the stool on a flat surface. All three feet should contact simultaneously. If one rocks, mark the high foot and trim 1–2 mm at a time until level.

  8. Final trim — With stool upright, scribe around each foot parallel to the floor (use a small block as a height gauge). Saw feet to the scribed line so they sit flat when splayed.

  9. Sand and finish — Sand seat face through 80 → 120 → 180 grit. Break all arrises. Apply oil or wax for a shop stool, or leave bare.

Notes

  • Three legs are inherently stable — no adjustment needed for uneven floors.
  • If a mortise blows out (too close to the edge), fill with a dowel plug and re-bore slightly inward.
  • Draw-boring option: after dry-fitting, remove leg, drill a 6 mm hole through the seat directly over the mortise, then re-insert tenon and mark through the seat hole onto the tenon. Remove and drill a 6 mm hole in the tenon ~1 mm closer to the shoulder. Re-assemble and drive a tapered hardwood peg — the offset draws the joint tight mechanically, no clamps needed.
