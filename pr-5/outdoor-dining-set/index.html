Build steps

Work order: benches first, then table. Benches use the same joinery at a smaller, more forgiving scale.

Benches (repeat for each)

  1. Mill all parts — Face and edge all stock. Cut legs to exact length. Mark a reference face and edge on each leg.

  2. Lay out mortises — On the inner face of each leg, mark two mortises: 15 mm wide × 55 mm tall, centred on apron height (90 mm), positioned flush with the top of the leg. Mark all 8 bench legs before cutting any.

  3. Chop or rout mortises — Chop in stages with mortise chisel, working from each end toward the middle. Or rout with a straight bit and MDF template. Clean walls and square corners. Depth: 17 mm (tenon thickness + 2 mm clearance).

  4. Cut tenons — Mark tenon shoulders at 50 mm from each apron end (= leg width). Tenon: 15 mm thick × 55 mm tall, centred on apron. Saw cheeks with tenon saw; saw shoulders; pare with shoulder plane until tenon enters mortise with firm hand pressure.

  5. Dry-fit each bench end frame — Front leg + rear leg + short apron. All shoulders must contact the leg face. Check for square.

  6. Glue up end frames — Apply PVA to mortise walls and tenon faces. Clamp across the frame with a bar clamp. Measure diagonals; adjust with clamp angle if out of square. Let cure overnight.

  7. Connect end frames with long aprons — Glue and clamp the two long aprons between the end frames. Clamp across bench length. Check for twist (sight along length — all four legs should contact a flat surface simultaneously).

  8. Glue up seat panel — Prepare and edge-joint three seat boards. Glue with cauls; cure overnight. Flatten, trim to size.

  9. Fit breadboard ends — Glue and pin at centre only; slot outer fixings.

  10. Fix seat to frame with wooden buttons — Rout 5 mm groove on apron inner top edge; cut and screw buttons.

Table

  1. Repeat steps 1–7 at table scale — Mortises 15 mm wide × 55 mm tall in 90 × 90 legs; tenons same profile. Table short aprons are only 900 mm so still manageable.

  2. Glue up tabletop — 5 boards × 160 mm = 800 mm net width. Use 4 pipe clamps alternating top and bottom to prevent bowing; cauls between clamps and boards. Apply PVA to edges only (not face). Cure overnight.

  3. Flatten top — Scrape glue lines flush with a cabinet scraper. Belt sand at 45° to grain then with grain, through 80 → 120 grit. Hand plane any remaining ridges.

  4. Trim to final size — Track saw or circular saw with guide to square and trim the panel to 1700 mm × 800 mm before breadboards.

  5. Fit breadboard ends — Centre hole: 10 mm round tenon or loose spline, glued; outer two holes: 8 mm slots routed in breadboard, screws not glued. Allows up to ±5 mm movement.

  6. Fix top to table frame — Same button method as benches; rout groove in aprons.

  7. Sand and finish entire set — Sand through 120 → 180 grit. Apply 2–3 coats outdoor oil, wiping off excess each time. Allow 24 h between coats. Pay special attention to end grain (apply double).

Notes

  • Assembly sequence is critical: complete and cure both end frames before adding long aprons. Gluing all eight joints simultaneously causes racking.
  • Twist check: after connecting long aprons, set assembled frame on a known-flat surface (or use winding sticks) and confirm all four legs contact. Correct by planing a leg foot or re-clamping.
  • Glue-up window: exterior PVA has a 15–20 min open time at 20 °C — enough for two end frames but not all six joints at once.
  • Wood movement: a 900 mm-wide hardwood panel moves ≈4–6 mm seasonally. The slotted breadboard ends and wooden buttons are non-optional if you want a flat, crack-free top in 10 years.
  • Refinishing: oil finish can be recoated after light sanding when it weathers; plan to re-oil once a year in the first two years, then as needed.
