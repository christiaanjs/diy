# Firewood Shelter — Build Plan

## Tools required

### Power tools
- Circular saw (or mitre saw) — framing cuts
- Drill/driver — screws throughout
- SDS hammer drill or angle grinder — driving rebar pegs
- Jigsaw — optional, trimming paling

### Hand tools
- Tape measure and square
- Level (1.2 m or longer)
- String line and pegs — layout
- Hammer — nailing paling and noggins
- Sledgehammer — driving rebar
- Clamps × 4 — holding plates while fixing
- Pencil and marking knife

### Jigs & accessories
- Post holder / post level — plumbing posts
- Saw horses × 2
- Safety glasses, hearing protection, gloves

## Build steps

1. **Set out the site** — Mark the 2400 × 2600 mm footprint with string lines. Check square by measuring diagonals (should match). Confirm the ground is reasonably level; pack low spots if needed.

2. **Position sleepers** — Lay the three sleepers parallel, running front-to-back (2400 mm direction), at 1200 mm centre-to-centre. Outermost sleeper faces are 2600 mm apart. Check they are level with each other; use a long level and packing.

3. **Drive rebar pegs** — Drive two 1200 mm rebar pegs beside each sleeper end and the mid-sleeper, positioning them tight against the timber face. Leave ~400 mm proud. Drive 8 pegs total (refer to model for positions: outer sleeper corners and middle sleeper ends). Confirm sleepers cannot shift.

4. **Cut posts to length** — Cut 3 front posts to 1800 mm and 3 back posts to 1400 mm. Treat cut ends with H4 end-grain preservative. Label F (front) and B (back).

5. **Erect and plumb posts** — Stand each post on the centreline of its sleeper at the front and back edges. Brace temporarily, check plumb on two faces. Fix with 75 × 75 galv angle brackets (2 per post base, one each side). Toe-nail through bracket with 40 mm joist hanger nails.

6. **Fix front wall plate** — Cut the 2600 mm front plate from 90 × 45. Rest it flat (90 mm vertical) on top of the three front posts, flush with the outer faces. Clamp, level, then fix with 2 × angle brackets per post and structural screws through the bracket into the post top. The plate overhangs 50 mm each side of the outer posts.

7. **Fix back wall plate** — Repeat for the back plate on the three back posts. The back plate sits ~400 mm lower than the front — this creates the skillion slope. Confirm the height difference is 400 mm front-to-back at each post pair before fixing.

8. **Cut and fix rafters** — Cut five rafters to 2878 mm (along-slope). Position: two edge rafters flush with the outer sleeper faces, then three evenly spaced at 600 mm centres. Rest each rafter on top of the wall plates, running front-to-back, with 300 mm overhang at the front and 150 mm at the rear. Fix with angle brackets at each end (2 brackets per rafter end). The rafter will angle slightly with the slope — the bracket allows this.

9. **Cut and fix back-wall noggins** — Cut four noggins to 1100 mm (clear span between posts less the 100 mm post width). Fix two rows per bay at 1/3 and 2/3 of back post height (≈ 467 mm and 933 mm above sleeper top). Face-screw or nail-through from post face into noggin end; two fixings per joint.

10. **Nail back-wall paling** — Trim 28 paling boards from 1500 mm to 1450 mm (full back post height). Start from one end, butt boards tight vertically. Nail each board top and bottom through to the wall plate and bottom noggin, and through the two intermediate noggins (4 fixings per board). Keep boards plumb as you work across.

11. **Fix roof sheeting** — Lay four 810 × 3300 mm corrugated iron sheets across the rafters, starting from the low (rear) end and working upward so each sheet laps over the one below. Lap sheets 1.5 corrugations side-to-side. Fix with 65 mm hex-head roofing screws into every rafter, screwing through the crown of the corrugation. Fit ridge flashing or turn up the top sheet if desired.

12. **Final checks and treatment** — Apply timber preservative to any untreated cut ends. Check all brackets are fully nailed/screwed. Verify the roof is correctly sloped away from the stack (water runs to the back). Clear offcuts and pack the shelter with firewood.

## Notes

- **Assembly order** — erect all posts before fixing any plates; fix both plates before cutting rafters so you can measure the actual along-slope dimension if anything has shifted.
- **Post plumb** — diagonal bracing with scrap timber between opposite posts during erection keeps things square until the plates are on.
- **Grain/end treatment** — all H4 cut ends must be treated; preservative takes 30 min to dry before assembly.
- **Roof screws** — drive into the crest of each corrugation, not the valley; use the neoprene-washer hex screws included in the box.
- **Paling airflow** — butt-jointed (no gaps) is fine; the open front provides adequate airflow for seasoning. Alternatively leave 5 mm gaps for extra ventilation.
- **Rebar alternative** — if the ground is rocky, M10 × 100 coach screws can be driven down through pre-drilled sleeper holes at an angle as a softer alternative to rebar.
