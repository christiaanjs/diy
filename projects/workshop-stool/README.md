# Workshop Stool

A low three-legged shop stool with a round seat and splayed round legs, mortised into the underside of the seat. Three-legged stools are inherently stable on uneven floors and are the classic first mortise-and-tenon project — small enough that errors are cheap, complex enough that the skills transfer directly to furniture.

## Status
**planning**

## Dimensions
- Seat: 280 mm diameter × 40 mm thick
- Seat height: 450 mm from floor
- Legs: 35 mm diameter round stock, ~10° outward splay
- Tenons: 22 mm diameter × 30 mm long, round, blind into seat

## Materials
- Primary: pine glulam blank or solid hardwood (seat); hardwood dowel (legs)
- Adhesive: exterior PVA

## Joinery
- **Round mortise and tenon** — 22 mm dia tenon bored into seat underside with a brace and bit or drill press; legs splayed outward 10° using an angled boring guide or wedged bevel gauge

## Notes
- This is the first M&T project in the progression — round tenons are easier than rectangular because they require only one operation (boring) rather than four-sided layout and chiselling
- Splay angle matters: too steep and the stool is wobbly; too vertical and it looks awkward. 10° is a classic value
- Draw-bore option: drill a 6 mm hole through the seat and tenon (slightly offset), drive a tapered wooden peg to draw the joint tight without clamps
- Once comfortable with round M&T here, the rectangular M&T in the garden-bench and outdoor-side-table projects will feel natural
