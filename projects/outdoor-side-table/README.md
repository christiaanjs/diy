# Outdoor Side Table

A compact outdoor side table (600 × 400 × 700 mm) with a four-leg apron frame and a glued-up solid timber top with breadboard ends. This project introduces the two skills that are directly prerequisite for the dining table: edge-joining multiple boards into a solid panel, and fixing a solid top to a frame in a way that allows seasonal wood movement.

## Status
**planning**

## Dimensions
- Overall: 600 W × 400 D × 700 H mm
- Top: 600 × 400 × 20 mm (3 edge-joined boards + 2 breadboard ends)
- Legs: 50 × 50 mm, 680 mm long
- Aprons: 70 × 22 mm

## Materials
- Primary: cedar solid boards (top), cedar section timber (legs, aprons)
- Fasteners: pocket-hole screws + wooden buttons (top to apron — allows movement)
- Adhesive: exterior PVA (apron glue-up; top panel edge joints)

## Joinery
- **Mortise and tenon** — apron tenons into leg mortises (four-corner frame)
- **Edge joint** — three boards face-planed, edges jointed or hand-planed flat, glued with PVA and cauls
- **Breadboard ends** — cross-grain end pieces glued only at centre, slotted screws at ends to allow ±3 mm seasonal movement
- **Wooden buttons / pocket screws** — top fixed to apron with movement-allowing fasteners

## Notes
- The glued-up top panel is the key new skill: get edges square and flat before gluing (a hand plane on a shooting board works well at this scale); use cauls to prevent buckling under clamp pressure
- Breadboard ends keep the top flat over time; the centre pin is glued, outer slots are not — critical detail to get right
- At 600 mm wide, this panel is small enough to manage solo with two pipe clamps
- Haunched tenons would be typical at this corner (the haunch fills the groove if a groove is used for a panel); for a solid apron frame, plain tenons are fine
